Bertrand Drouvot

March 23, 2026 by Eren Turkoglu

Interviews PostgreSQL Open Source AWS

Can you introduce yourself?

My name is Bertrand Drouvot. I have been working around databases for about 25 years. I spent a long time with Oracle, then fell in love with Postgres 6 or 7 years ago. Since then, I joined an AWS team dedicated to PostgreSQL contributions, and that is now my main activity.

Did PostgreSQL contributions help you join AWS?

No. I first joined AWS as a consultant helping customers migrate their databases to AWS. Then I moved into an RDS service team. About three and a half years ago, a dedicated PostgreSQL contribution team was created. I applied and got in.

What is your academic background?

I studied mathematics first, then continued in math and computer science through a postgraduate degree.

Do you still use things you learned at university?

Yes. During that program I learned database fundamentals, and that still matters. I also learned C.

How did you discover databases?

It was my first assignment at the beginning of my career while working at a services company. I enjoyed it, so I stayed in the field. What makes it interesting is that it touches everything: systems, networking, security, and software development. It is a very cross-functional domain where you can invest in several aspects at once.

How did you get into open source?

It happened through both chance and interest. I worked with Oracle for a long time, but the black-box side of it wore me out. I already enjoyed sharing scripts with the Oracle community. When I discovered Postgres, I got serious about it and started building extensions. Later, at AWS, I was in database engineering, the last line of support, so we sometimes hit bugs directly. My first PostgreSQL contribution was a bug fix, and most of my work has been around logical decoding.

What impact does open source have on your work?

Today it is 100% of my work. I only contribute to PostgreSQL. I sometimes contribute to Linux when we identify bugs elsewhere, but overall my work is fully centered on open source.

What is your advice for getting started in open source?

The most important thing is to find a domain you genuinely care about, not to contribute just for the sake of it. When you work with a product, you will inevitably run into bugs or things to improve, and that is the best place to start.

What does your debugging toolbox look like?

I use `git bisect` to identify the commit that introduced a bug. For memory issues, Valgrind is useful. I also use GDB with breakpoints and especially watchpoints. My environment is built around tmux, provisioning scripts, and NeoVim with clang tools.

What do you think about the impact of AI?

There are positives and negatives. On the positive side, it boosts productivity for small functions, test scripts, or reading assembly code. On the negative side, we also see patches generated without real understanding, which is a problem in open source.

How do you spend your free time?

I enjoy TV series, I watch a lot of basketball, especially European and French leagues, and I go on motorcycle rides with friends.
